I am an AI researcher from Nepal dedicated to human-computer interaction and digital health. My core objective is to study how intelligent systems can adapt to human needs — and my ongoing work looks for ways AI can bridge human emotion and technology to improve clinical and mental health outcomes.

I hold a Bachelor's degree in Computer Science and Engineering from the Vellore Institute of Technology, where I majored in data science, graduated in 2025 with a CGPA of 8.63 out of 10, and made the Dean's List for academic research. I have since expanded that footprint as a visiting research student at Deakin University's Tulip Lab, a research affiliate at Monash University, and a collaborator with the Earth Observation & AI group at the University of Melbourne.

The through-line is a preference for structure over scale. Most of the problems I care about come with small datasets — an fMRI cohort of a few hundred subjects, an EEG recording session too short for a plain transformer — and a clinical question that a black box is not allowed to answer alone. So I build with structured priors instead of more data: adjacency that is learned rather than assumed, quantum-circuit parameterisations that carry some of the inductive bias, and attribution that lets a prediction be traced back to a specific physiological or environmental driver.

About Utsav Poudel

Utsav Poudel is an AI researcher from Nepal working in human-computer interaction, affective computing and digital health. He holds a Bachelor's degree from the Vellore Institute of Technology, is a visiting research student at Deakin University's Tulip Lab, collaborates with the Earth Observation & AI group at the University of Melbourne, and founded Fulcrum, a non-profit providing free AI education.

How intelligent systems can adapt to human needs. In practice that means working with physiological and neurological signals — EEG, fMRI — using graph neural networks, attention-based models and hybrid quantum–classical architectures, applied to affective computing, mental health and clinical decision support.

In Issues in Mental Health Nursing and Psychiatry (both Taylor & Francis), the Journal of Circuits, Systems and Computers (World Scientific), and at IEEE CINS 2025 — with preprints on arXiv and two patents filed with Intellectual Property India. The work has been cited more than 75 times.

A volunteer-run non-profit he founded in 2025 in Kathmandu. It offers free AI mentorship, research and publication support, project building, workshops and a guest speaker series, and has reached more than 200 students across rural Nepal and the Indian subcontinent. Everything it publishes stays free.
